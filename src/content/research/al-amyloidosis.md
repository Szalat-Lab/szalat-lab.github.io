title: Plasma cell biology in AL amyloidosis
order: 1
featured: true
summary: >-
  In AL amyloidosis, a small clone of plasma cells produces misfolded light chains that damage the heart,
  kidneys, and other organs. We use single-cell profiling of patient bone marrow to study the clonal plasma
  cells that drive the disease alongside the non-malignant plasma cells that surround them.
questions:
  - How do clonal plasma cells in AL amyloidosis differ from normal plasma cells and from myeloma cells?
  - Which programs allow a small clone to produce large amounts of toxic light chain?
  - How do polyclonal plasma cells and the immune system respond to the presence of the clone?
approaches:
  - Single-cell RNA sequencing of bone marrow plasma cells
  - Paired immune repertoire (BCR) profiling to separate clonal and polyclonal cells
  - Integrative multiomic analysis
  - Patient cohorts from the Boston Medical Center Amyloidosis Center
